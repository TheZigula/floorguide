# MM-P102 Hydraulic Press P-102 Maintenance Manual

Northbridge Fabrication, Plant 2, Line 3. Rev C, issued 2026-05-04. This manual
is the controlled source for P-102 service intervals. Any card, sheet, or extract
taken from it is a copy and does not carry current intervals; where a copy and
this manual differ, this manual is correct. Safety procedures SP-01, SP-03, and
SP-04 outrank this manual on anything to do with energy isolation or guarding.

## 1. Machine data

150-ton hydraulic press, 55 kW pump, 180 litre reservoir, nitrogen accumulator,
light curtain and two-hand control per SP-04. The hour meter on the main panel
counts pump running hours only.

## 3. Service intervals

Intervals are in pump operating hours, counted from the meter reading recorded at
the last service of that item. They are not calendar based.

| Item | Interval | Reference |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Hydraulic filter, replace element | every 250 operating hours | Sec 3.1 |
| Rod and cylinder seal inspection | every 500 operating hours | Sec 3.4 |
| Hydraulic oil, sample and change | every 1000 operating hours | Sec 3.6 |
| Accumulator pre-charge check | every 1000 operating hours | Sec 3.7 |
| Light curtain function test | every shift, per SP-04 | SP-04 |

### 3.1 Hydraulic filter

Replace the filter element every 250 operating hours. Record the meter reading in
the service log at the moment of the change; the next due point is that reading
plus 250. A filter left in past its interval opens its internal relief and sends
unfiltered oil to the pump. Running past the interval is the most common cause of
pump wear on this machine.

## 4. Symptoms and first checks

### 4.2 Grinding or growling noise from the pump

A grinding, growling, or gravelly noise from the pump area means pump cavitation:
the pump is starved of oil and is drawing vapour bubbles that collapse against
the pump internals. Work through these in order.

1. Check the hydraulic filter first. A restricted or overdue filter element is
   the most common cause of cavitation on P-102. Check the service log for hours
   since the last filter change and replace the element if it is at or past 250
   operating hours.
2. Check the reservoir oil level at the sight glass with the ram down. A low
   level uncovers the suction strainer.
3. Check oil viscosity and temperature. Cold, thick oil cavitates on start-up;
   let the machine warm up before running production.
4. Check the suction line and its clamps for an air leak, and the strainer for
   sludge.

Do not keep running the press while it cavitates. Sustained cavitation destroys
the pump in hours, not weeks. Lock the machine out under SP-01 and bleed the
accumulator before opening the hydraulic enclosure for any of these checks.
