# SP-03 Emergency Stop and Restart After a Fault

Northbridge Fabrication, Plant 2, Line 3. This procedure governs restart after an
emergency stop, an interlock trip, or a machine fault. It outranks any
maintenance manual, quick-reference card, or tip sheet.

## 1. Immediately after the stop

Leave the emergency stop latched. Do not reset the fault at the panel and do not
cycle power to clear an alarm. Tell the line supervisor. Note the machine, the
time, and what the machine was doing.

## 2. Before anyone enters the guarded area

Lockout/tagout under SP-01 is applied and verified first, including the try-out
step and, on P-102, bleeding the accumulator to zero. An emergency stop is not an
energy isolation. It stops motion; it does not remove stored hydraulic pressure
or electrical energy.

## 3. Finding the cause

The cause of the stop is identified and recorded before the machine is released.
A fault that cleared by itself is still recorded. If the cause is a guard, a light
curtain, a two-hand control, or any other interlock, the machine stays down until
the device is repaired and tested.

## 4. Interlocks are never defeated

An interlock is never blocked, taped, jumpered, wired around, or bypassed to get
the line running, for any reason, for any length of time, at any production
target. A defeated interlock is a stop-work condition and the machine is locked
out until the defeat is removed and the device is tested.

## 5. Restart

Restart requires, in order: the cause recorded; the repair or correction done;
every guard refitted; the guarded area confirmed clear; every lock and tag
removed by its owner; and the line supervisor's sign-off in the restart log.
Production pressure is never a reason to shorten this list. There is no partial
restart and no trial cycle before sign-off.
